A forest that grows into the mud

Roots in salt water, carbon stacked metres deep below them.

How it stores carbon Why it counts as a credit

Where does a mangrove keep its carbon?

Mostly underground. Across 25 Indo-Pacific forests, the soil held between 49% and 98% of the total store.

Does it keep adding more?

Yes. Tides bring in sediment and the forest buries carbon in it every year, about 139 grams a square metre on average.

Million tonnes of carbon buried a year, worldwide. Bars are the estimate, lines the 95% interval.

Setting matters: mangroves on carbonate coasts bury less than those fed by river sediment.

What is clearing them?

Coastal development, aquaculture ponds and over-harvesting. Between them, the world lost 30 to 50% of its mangrove area in half a century.

What else does a mangrove do?

It holds up the fishery and cycles nutrients through the creek. Lose the forest and the catch goes with the carbon.

Protect the forest, keep the store.
