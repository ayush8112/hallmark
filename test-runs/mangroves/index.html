How credits work

What is a mangrove actually holding?

More carbon per hectare than most tropical forests, mostly in the mud.

See the numbers Read the study

So how much carbon, exactly?

1,023

tonnes of carbon in one hectare, on average.

Measured across 25 mangrove forests in the Indo-Pacific, trees, dead wood and soil together. That puts mangroves among the most carbon-rich forests in the tropics.

Wait, the carbon is in the mud?

Yes. The soil under a mangrove runs from half a metre to more than three metres deep, and it holds most of the store.

Share of the carbon held in soil, lowest forest to highest.

What happens if we clear them?

The mud stops being a vault. Cleared mangroves release their store, and they are being cleared fast.

How does that become a carbon credit?

A credit is one tonne of carbon dioxide kept out of the air. Carbon is weighed as carbon; credits count CO2, which is 3.67 times heavier.

One hectare, on averageTonnes
Carbon stored1,023
As CO2 equivalent3,751

That stock is what protection keeps in the ground. Credits are only issued for what a certified project can measure and prove.

Why should the coast care?

The same forests feed the fishery and cycle nutrients for the creek. Keep the trees and the catch, the shoreline and the carbon stay together.
